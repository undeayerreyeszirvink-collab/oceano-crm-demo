# Persona Blueprint: Humanized Output for Digital Employees

Read this reference when the user wants the digital employee to feel like a vivid, understandable working role — not a checklist of capabilities.

## Design Principle

A virtual employee is not a function bundle. It is a **work persona** with a job to be done, a place in the organization, a style of collaboration, limits of authority, and recognizable moments of value.

The output should help a reader answer five intuitive questions:

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Who is this digital employee?** | Creates instant mental anchoring |
| **What job does it wake up to do every day?** | Makes scope concrete |
| **How does it work with people and systems?** | Makes collaboration real |
| **Where are its boundaries and handoff points?** | Builds trust and governance |
| **What does success look like in action?** | Makes value visible |

---

## The Six Modules

Do not present the result as a single capability list. Organize it as a compact **character dossier + operating blueprint**.

| # | Module | Purpose | Tone |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Role Portrait** | Introduce the digital employee as a concrete role | Vivid, human-readable, memorable |
| 2 | **A Day at Work** | Show what it handles across a working cycle | Narrative, situational, operational |
| 3 | **Responsibility Boundaries** | Clarify what it owns, assists, and does not do | Sharp, bounded, trustworthy |
| 4 | **Collaboration Map** | How it works with humans, systems, and approvals | Relational, realistic, governable |
| 5 | **Ability Blueprint** | Translate role into skills, knowledge, tools, decision patterns | Structured, implementation-ready |
| 6 | **Value and Guardrails** | Expected outcomes, risks, and red lines | Executive-friendly, confidence-building |

**Sequence matters: start human, then become structured.**

## Module 1: Role Portrait

Make the digital employee easy to picture. If this role joined the company tomorrow, how would you introduce it?

| Field | How to express |
|---|---|
| **Role name** | Concrete title — e.g. "渠道运营协调员", "售前线索分诊员", "Supply Chain Alert Coordinator" |
| **One-sentence mission** | Plain language, not technical jargon |
| **Why this role exists** | The business tension it resolves |
| **Work personality** | Style: steady, fast, meticulous, service-minded, etc. |
| **Typical triggers** | The moments when it starts working |

**Good example:**

> **线索分诊员 Lina** 是一位 24 小时在线的销售前端协调员。她的使命不是"回答所有问题"，而是先把新线索接住、分清轻重缓急、补齐关键背景，再把真正值得跟进的机会交到销售手里。

**This is stronger than:**

> The system classifies leads and routes them.

The difference: the first version has a name, a working posture, a mission framed as "what I do for the team", and a clear handoff. The second is a feature description that could be anything.

## Module 2: A Day at Work

Give the role a working rhythm. Show how it moves through a typical business loop.

This should read like a compact operating story, not a feature list.

| Moment | What to describe |
|---|---|
| **Start of work** | What signals wake it up |
| **Early actions** | What it checks, gathers, or clarifies first |
| **Middle of work** | How it processes, decides, or coordinates |
| **Escalation moment** | When it asks a human to step in |
| **Completion** | What output it leaves behind |

**Good example:**

> 每当官网、表单、企微或活动页有新线索进入，Lina 会先读懂来源、行业、问题类型与历史接触记录。她不会急着"回复模板"，而是先判断这是一次咨询、一次比价，还是一个真正有机会推进的项目。如果信息不完整，她会补问最关键的两三件事；如果线索已经成熟，她会把整理好的背景、建议优先级和下一步动作一起交给销售。

---

## Module 3: Responsibility Boundaries

Separate into three layers — more intuitive than a flat task list because it shows responsibility gradients:

| Layer | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Owns** | Work it can complete independently |
| **Assists** | Work it supports but does not finalize |
| **Never owns** | Work that must stay with a human |

Present as a three-column table so boundaries are immediately scannable.

---

## Module 4: Collaboration Map

A digital employee should never appear to work in a vacuum. Show who it partners with and how.

| Interface | What to show |
|---|---|
| **With people** | Who it serves, informs, or escalates to |
| **With systems** | Which systems it reads from or writes to |
| **With process rules** | Which approvals, thresholds, or policies shape its behavior |

**Good framing:**

> 她像一个坐在团队前台与后台之间的协调角色：前面接住需求，后面连接 CRM、知识库与销售流程，关键节点再请真人拍板。

---

## Module 5: Ability Blueprint

Only after the role feels vivid should you translate into structured design modules.

| Section | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Scene recognition** | What scenarios it must distinguish |
| **Knowledge base** | What knowledge it needs to work well |
| **Actions** | What actions it can take in systems or workflows |
| **Decision rules** | What thresholds or logic govern its choices |
| **Memory** | What context it remembers within and across tasks |
| **Output forms** | What artifacts it produces — summaries, drafts, tickets, recommendations |

This section maps clearly back to the Role Portrait. Every ability should trace to a moment in "A Day at Work".

---

## Module 6: Value and Guardrails

Make business value and the trust model explicit. Two visible parts:

| Part | What to express |
|---|---|
| **Value signals** | Faster response, better consistency, reduced coordination load, improved conversion, cleaner data |
| **Guardrails** | Non-scope, approval-required actions, sensitive cases, auditability, fallback rules |

**Good phrasing:**

> 她带来的核心价值，不是"替代销售"，而是把销售最不该浪费时间的前段杂务稳定接住。

> 她的红线也很清楚：不承诺价格，不拍板折扣，不替真人做最终商机判断。

## Document Structure Template

When writing a polished deliverable:

```
# [Name] — [Role Title]

[Short paragraph that makes the role immediately understandable]

## 1. Role Portrait
[Role name, mission, business reason, work personality, triggers]

## 2. A Day at Work
[Realistic working loop or representative workday narrative]

## 3. Responsibility Boundaries
[Three-column table: owns / assists / never owns]

## 4. Collaboration Map
[How the role connects to people, systems, and process checkpoints]

## 5. Ability Blueprint
[Scenes, knowledge, actions, decision rules, memory, outputs]

## 6. Value and Guardrails
[Expected value, risk boundaries, human takeover rules]
```

## Style Rules

| Rule | Instruction |
|---|---|
| **Start human, then structure** | Make the role easy to imagine before becoming technical |
| **Work language, not AI language** | Say what job it does, not what model it runs on |
| **Collaboration, not isolation** | A digital employee lives in a team context |
| **Visible boundaries** | Trust grows when non-scope is explicit |
| **Scenes over abstractions** | Readers believe examples more than generalizations |

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Why it weakens the output |
|---|---|
| Long capability list with no role identity | Generic and hard to remember |
| Pure org-chart language with no day-in-the-life view | Static and abstract |
| Human-like branding with no governance details | Unsafe or superficial |
| Technical architecture before role narrative | Non-technical stakeholders disengage |

## Quick Transformation

If you inherit a flat artifact, convert it:

| Flat element | Humanized replacement |
|---|---|
| Capability list → | Role Portrait + Responsibility Boundaries |
| Process steps → | A Day at Work narrative |
| Systems integration list → | Collaboration Map |
| Functional requirements → | Ability Blueprint |
| Risks and limitations → | Guardrails |
